Chromatic Adaptation

The chromatic adaptation algorithms on this web site may all be implemented as a linear transformation of a source color (XSXS, YSYS, ZSZS) into a destination color (XDXD, YDYD, ZDZD) by a linear transformation [M][M] which is dependent on the source reference white (XWSXWS, YWSYWS, ZWSZWS) and the destination reference white (XWDXWD, YWDYWD, ZWDZWD):

⎡⎢⎣XDYDZD⎤⎥⎦=[M]⎡⎢⎣XSYSZS⎤⎥⎦(1)(1)[XDYDZD]=[M][XSYSZS]

The idea behind all of these algorithms is to follow three steps:

  1. Transform from XYZ into a cone response domain, (ρρ, γγ, ββ).
  2. Scale the vector components by factors dependent upon both the source and destination reference whites.
  3. Transform from (ρρ, γγ, ββ) back to XYZ using the inverse transform of step 1.

These steps are represented by the three matrices shown here on the right:

[M]=[MA]−1⎡⎢⎣ρD/ρS000γD/γS000βD/βS⎤⎥⎦[MA](2)(2)[M]=[MA]−1[ρD/ρS000γD/γS000βD/βS][MA] ⎡⎢⎣ρSγSβS⎤⎥⎦=[MA]⎡⎢⎣XWSYWSZWS⎤⎥⎦(3)(3)[ρSγSβS]=[MA][XWSYWSZWS] ⎡⎢⎣ρDγDβD⎤⎥⎦=[MA]⎡⎢⎣XWDYWDZWD⎤⎥⎦(4)(4)[ρDγDβD]=[MA][XWDYWDZWD]

Three methods are presented here: XYZ Scaling, Bradford and Von Kries. The differences among the three methods lie in the definition of the cone response domains, [MA][MA].

XYZ Scaling is generally considered to be an inferior chromatic adaptation algorithm. Incidentally, this is the method that would result from transforming the source XYZ color to Lab using (XWSXWS, YWSYWS, ZWSZWS) followed by conversion back to XYZ using (XWDXWD, YWDYWD, ZWDZWD).

The Bradford method is the newest of the three methods, and is considered by most experts to be the best of them. This is the method used in Adobe Photoshop. A related article comparing the chromatic adaptation algorithms may be found here.
